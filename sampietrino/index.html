The Manifesto

A stone, a ship, a table.

Between 1860 and 1950, nearly three million Italians left for Argentina. They carried their seeds, their stubbornness, and their Sunday recipes. In Buenos Aires, you can still feel it — in the way the city makes its coffee, in its devotion to pasta, in the vineyards of Mendoza planted with Italian varietals.

Sampietrino was born from that crossing. We take products from both sides of the Atlantic — things made with the same rigour, the same refusal of shortcuts — and we put them in a box. Every month, one theme. One table set between two cities.

The name comes from the cobblestones of Rome. But the spirit? It belongs to both.

— The Sampietrino Manifesto

History

Three million Italians. One taste at a time.

This is not a food company. This is the return trip.

  1. 01

    1860–1950

    Three million Italians emigrate to Argentina from Calabria, Veneto, Campania, and Lazio. They carry their food and their habits — and they stay.

  2. 02

    LA BOCA, BUENOS AIRES

    Genoese workers build a neighbourhood and paint the houses with leftover boat paint. They argue about pasta. They never quite become Argentine.

  3. 03

    MENDOZA, 1900

    Italian winemakers plant the vineyards. Malbec — a French grape — transforms under their hands into something unmistakably Argentine.

  4. 04

    TODAY

    Sampietrino brings both sides together. One box. Two cultures. Every month, a different story.

The Experience

A box, not a parcel.

It arrives in a cement-grey cardboard box, sealed with a terracotta wax stamp — the colour of the stone it's named after.

Inside, each item is wrapped individually in écru tissue paper. On top: a handwritten story card — not printed, not laminated — written by whoever packed it that day, at that hour. It opens slowly. It's designed that way.

  1. 01 The seal breaks — it doesn't peel.
  2. 02 The tissue opens, one layer at a time.
  3. 03 The card is read before cooking.
  4. 04 The QR is scanned when you're ready to begin.
